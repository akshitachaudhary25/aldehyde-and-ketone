ALDEHYDE AND KETONE


ALDEHYDES
Aldehyde
1. IUPAC- Suffix 'al', for example butanal, propanal etc.
2. Many struuctures have common names like vanillin, salicaldehyde, cinnamaldehyde etc

Methods of preparation
1. Oxidation of alcohols
2o alcohol + Cu + heat _______> aldehyde
Reagents
  • PCC
  • PDC
  • Sarett's reagent
  • Collin's reagent

All the above mentioned reagents are mild reagents that convert alcohols to aldehydes. Strong oxidising reagents are not used for this reaction because aldehydes then get oxidised to acids. To stop the reaction at aldehydes we need to use a mild reagent.
2. Ozonolysis
C=C (bond) + O3 _______> ozonoid __Zn or H2/Pd_____> C=O + O=C + ZnO
3. Hydration of alkynes
HC≡CH + H2O __H2SO4/HgSO4___> CH2=CH-OH ___tautomerism__> CH3-CH=O
The reaction involves formationn of unstable enol in the second step which tautomerises to acetaldehyde whjich is much more stable by 1,3H+ shift. The reaction follows markonikove rule (rich becomes richer) and no formation of carbocation.
Points to remember
  • Formaldehydes cannot be prepared i.e HCHO.
  • Methyl ketones is always major.
  • Acetaldehyde is the only aldehyde formed.
